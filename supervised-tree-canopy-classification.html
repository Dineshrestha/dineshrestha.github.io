Tree Canopy Classification with Agriculture-Aware Refinement

A high-resolution remote-sensing workflow that compares supervised classifiers, measures tree canopy along quarter-mile pipeline segments, and applies a carefully tested agriculture mask to reduce false-positive canopy.

Tree canopy was the target. Agricultural confusion was the obstacle.

High-resolution imagery captured tree crowns well, but crops, field edges, and other vegetation could resemble canopy spectrally. The challenge was not simply producing a classified raster—it was building a defensible process that measured canopy consistently and corrected known agricultural false positives without erasing legitimate trees.

01 / CLASSIFICATION

No single model wins automatically

Random Trees, Support Vector Machine, and Minimum Distance classifications were compared through validation and visual review.

02 / SCALE

Fine imagery, long corridor

Sub-meter imagery and project-wide coverage created large rasters, making processing design, resumability, and output validation essential.

03 / BOUNDARIES

Agriculture masks can overcorrect

An overly aggressive inward buffer could remove real windbreak, riparian, farmstead, and field-edge canopy.

Classification, correction, and reporting operate as one workflow.

The pilot used 2023 NAIP imagery and independent sample data to compare classifiers, select a working land-cover model, standardize the corridor into reporting segments, and refine agricultural correction through county-wide review.

CLASSIFIER SELECTION

Three approaches, one working model

Random Trees, Support Vector Machine, and Minimum Distance were tested against the same landscape problem. Minimum Distance was selected for the pilot and carried forward as a three-class product: Tree Canopy, Low Vegetation, and Non-Vegetated.

Accuracy assessment and visual interpretation were used together because a single overall statistic does not fully reveal where tree canopy is being confused with agricultural vegetation.

REPORTING FRAMEWORK

Canopy percentage that can be compared

The pipeline centerline was divided into standardized quarter-mile segments. Each segment used the same corridor definition and denominator, allowing canopy acres and canopy percentage to be compared consistently along the project.

This creates a stable unit for hotspot review, QA/QC, client reporting, and future reruns.

The mask was tested—not assumed.

Agricultural land was a significant source of false-positive canopy. Multiple refinement scenarios were evaluated to find a correction strong enough to remove that error while preserving real trees near field boundaries.

Baseline agriculture mask

A common 2024–2025 agriculture layer identified locations consistently mapped as agriculture across both years.

Buffer comparison

Original, 10 m, 15 m, and 20 m inward-mask scenarios were compared quantitatively and visually.

County-wide inspection

The complete pilot county was reviewed rather than relying on a few favorable examples.

10 m finding

The 10 m inward buffer performed best among the geometric buffer scenarios.

Raster-cell refinement

A one-cell shrink preserved straighter, grid-aligned boundaries and outperformed curved geometric inset edges during visual QA.

Final correction rule

Tree Canopy overlapping the refined agriculture mask becomes Low Vegetation; other classes remain unchanged.

PROJECT-WIDE PROCESSING

Large rasters require operational safeguards

  • Preserve each source raster’s CRS, resolution, extent, and NoData value
  • Process imagery in memory-conscious blocks
  • Write a partial output before final rename
  • Skip completed counties during restart
  • Preserve the State → FIPS folder structure
  • Report start/end time and masked-pixel counts
  • Run final input/output inventory checks

QA/QC

Review the places most likely to fail

  • Field edges and narrow windbreaks
  • Riparian vegetation and farmsteads
  • Known agricultural polygons
  • Mask alignment and NoData behavior
  • Before/after canopy acreage and percentage
  • Class-value preservation outside the mask
  • County completeness and readable outputs

RESULT

A repeatable canopy metric with a defensible correction strategy.

The pilot established a working supervised classification, quarter-mile reporting framework, and agriculture-mask method grounded in quantitative comparison and full-county visual QA. The one-cell shrink became the preferred correction because it reduced agricultural false positives while preserving cleaner field-edge geometry.
